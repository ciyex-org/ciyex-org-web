NABH runs two digital health programmes, and they are easy to confuse. One assesses how well a hospital uses digital systems. The other certifies software. Knowing the difference will help you plan, and help you judge what vendors tell you.

<!-- truncate -->

## Programme 1: accreditation for healthcare providers

NABH's Digital Health Accreditation Programme assesses how well a hospital uses digital systems to improve patient care, safety and efficiency. It is open to healthcare providers with an inpatient or day care setup.

According to NABH's FAQ:

- The standards have eight core chapters covering clinical and non-clinical areas
- Providers are assessed across four maturity levels: Core, Commitment, Achievement and Excellence
- Based on their performance, they receive Silver, Gold or Platinum status
- Accreditation is valid for two years, and reassessment is needed after that
- Accredited providers must apply for a main NABH accreditation programme in the third year, unless they already hold one

## Programme 2: certification for HIS and EMR software

This programme is for software products. NABH says a product needs at least three deployments in operational hospitals in India and a current ABDM M3 compliance certificate to be eligible. Certification is valid for two years.

The key point is that **a certified product does not make your hospital accredited**. Good software helps, but accreditation looks at how your hospital actually works.

## Questions to ask your software vendor

If a vendor mentions NABH, ask:

- Is the product certified, or does the claim only mean it can "support" the standards?
- Which edition and level does the certificate cover?
- Is the certificate still valid?
- Does it cover the HIS, the EMR or both?

NABH's website has a tool for verifying certificates.

## How to prepare your hospital

**Start with a gap review.** Read the current standards and compare them with how you work today. Check NABH's website for the current edition, as the standards are revised over time.

**Fix documentation habits first.** Clear policies and consistent records will help whichever level you aim for.

**Bring your departments together.** Clinical staff, IT and quality teams all need to take part. This is not only an IT project.

**Set a realistic target.** Pick a level that matches your hospital's current maturity, then plan upward from there.

**Review security and access.** Know who can see what, how activity is recorded, and how you back up data. This also supports your [DPDP Act](/blog/dpdp-act-what-hospitals-should-know) readiness.
