Most hospitals do not fail at going digital because of the software. They struggle because everything changes at once while patients keep arriving. A phased rollout lowers that risk and gives staff time to build confidence.

<!-- truncate -->

## Choose an owner

Name one person who can make decisions and resolve disputes between departments. Give each department a "champion": a respected staff member who learns the system early and helps colleagues on the floor. A project with no owner tends to stall.

## Clean up your master data first

A HIMS is only as good as the lists inside it. Before going live, prepare:

- Service and tariff lists
- Doctors, departments and consultation schedules
- Wards, rooms and beds
- Drug and store item lists, with current stock
- Laboratory test lists

This work is slow, but mistakes here turn into wrong bills and confused staff later.

## Roll out in stages

The right order depends on your hospital. A common approach is to follow the patient:

1. **Front desk:** registration, appointments and billing
2. **Outpatient care:** consultations and the queue
3. **Departments:** laboratory, radiology and pharmacy
4. **Inpatient care:** admissions, beds, nursing and theatre
5. **Records and administration:** discharge, medical records, stores and accounts

Finish and stabilise each stage before starting the next. If you are a smaller hospital, you may be able to combine stages.

## Decide what happens to old records

You rarely need to enter every old file on day one. Most hospitals decide on a cut-off date, register returning patients as they come back, and keep older paper files accessible. Before you dispose of anything, check the retention rules that apply in your state.

## Train on real tasks

Short sessions built around each person's daily work are more useful than a long tour of every screen. Let staff practise with test patients on a training setup until the common tasks feel routine.

## Plan the go-live day

- Choose a day with lower patient volume
- Keep extra support staff at the front desk and in billing
- Keep paper forms ready as a fallback
- Agree in advance who decides if you need to fall back
- Hold a short review at the end of each day in the first week

## Expect a slow first few weeks

Staff will be slower at first, and some patients may wait longer. Tell your teams this in advance, so that an early dip is not mistaken for failure. Track a few simple measures, such as registration time, billing errors and discharge time, so you can see real progress.
