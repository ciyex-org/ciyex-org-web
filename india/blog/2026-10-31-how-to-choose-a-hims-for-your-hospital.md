Buying a hospital information management system (HIMS) is a decision your staff will live with for years. A polished demo tells you little about how the system will behave at the front desk at 10 am on a busy Monday. These are the checks that matter more.

<!-- truncate -->

## Start with your patient's journey

Before you speak to any vendor, walk through one patient's visit in your own hospital: registration, consultation, tests, pharmacy, billing and, for inpatients, admission through discharge. Note every point where someone copies information by hand, makes a phone call or walks a paper file across the building.

That list becomes your requirement document. It keeps every demo focused on your problems, not the vendor's favourite features.

## Ask to see your workflow, not a script

In each demo, ask the vendor to run a case from your hospital. For example:

- A patient who starts in OPD, needs a lab test and is then admitted
- A bed transfer in the middle of an admission
- A bill correction after the final bill has been printed

Watch how many screens each task takes, and how the system handles the exception. Exceptions are where weak systems create workarounds.

## Put the people who will use it in the room

Invite a receptionist, a staff nurse, a pharmacist and someone from accounts. They will spot problems that a management team cannot see. If the system will be used on a shared ward computer, test it on one.

## Check what is available today

Vendors often describe features that are still being built. Ask a direct question for each item on your list: is this available today, is it in development, or is it planned? Ask for the answer in writing. Treat this as normal diligence, including with us.

## Look closely at the rollout

The software is only part of the project. Ask:

- Who trains your staff, and for how many days?
- How will existing patient data be moved?
- What support is available during the first weeks?
- What happens when the internet or power goes down?

## Be clear about your data

Patient records belong in your hospital's control. Before signing, confirm how you can export your data, in what format, and what happens to it if you leave. Also ask how access is controlled and how activity is recorded. Our guide to the [DPDP Act for hospitals](/blog/dpdp-act-what-hospitals-should-know) explains why this matters.

## Compare the total cost

Compare costs over several years, not just the first quote. Include licences, implementation, training, hardware, machine interfaces, support and future changes. A cheaper quote can become expensive if every change is billed separately.

## Check readiness for what comes next

If [ABDM](/blog/abdm-guide-for-hospitals) or [NABH digital health accreditation](/blog/nabh-digital-health-standards-explained) is part of your plan, ask how the vendor supports each, and ask for current evidence.
